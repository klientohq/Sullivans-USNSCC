U.S. Naval Sea Cadet Corps, Buffalo, New York

Join The Sullivans Division.

A youth program for ages 10 through high school. Cadets learn seamanship, drill, first aid, and how to lead people. There is no obligation to join the military.

What parents ask first

Does this obligate my child to join the military?
No. The Naval Sea Cadet Corps is a congressionally chartered non-profit youth program supported by the Navy and Coast Guard. It is not enlistment, and cadets incur no service obligation. Many cadets go on to college or a civilian career. That choice stays entirely theirs.
Who can join?

Young people roughly ages 10 through high school. The Navy League Cadet Corps covers the younger group; the Naval Sea Cadet Corps covers the older one. If your child is under 13, a parent or guardian completes the enrollment.

Beyond age, the program asks that a cadet is unmarried, enrolled in school, has a parent or guardian's consent, and is of good moral character. A medical examination is required, similar to a high school sports physical.

A medical condition is not a bar. No applicant is denied for a medical disability. Where a condition rules out full participation, a parent or guardian can submit a request for accommodation so the cadet takes part as fully as possible.

What does it cost?

A new Sea Cadet pays $150 in annual dues and $150 for the sea bag of issued kit, so roughly $300 in the first year. A League Cadet, the younger group, pays the same $150 in dues with a $75 sea bag, so roughly $225. Dues renew each year; the sea bag is a one-time cost.

Meals at drill are $5 for a day drill and $20 for a weekend drill.

Training is a separate cost, set nationally rather than by us. The national program publishes recruit training at around $300, which a new high school Sea Cadet attends in the first year, and later trainings at roughly $300 each with no cap on how many a cadet takes. Travel to a training site is not included. We tell you the figure before your cadet commits to anything.

If cost is the thing standing in the way, say so when you contact us. We would rather work it out with you than lose a cadet over it.

Where and when do you drill?
The division drills in the Buffalo area. We share exact dates and the meeting location directly with families who contact us, rather than publishing a standing schedule of where a group of minors will be.

How to enroll

Enrollment runs through the national Sea Cadets site.

Every unit in the country enrolls the same way. It takes a few minutes, and the division's recruiting team follows up with you directly.

  1. Open the national enrollment page

    It asks you to find a unit on a map and request information. Nothing is final at this stage.

  2. Select The Sullivans Division, Buffalo NY

    This is the step that matters. The map lists every unit in the region, and choosing a different one sends your request to another division instead of to us.

    Look for The Sullivans Division, Buffalo NY

  3. Submit the request, and we will reach out

    Our recruiting team contacts you to arrange a visit to a drill so you and your child can see the program before deciding anything.

    Nationally, most units take new cadets in September and January. Ask us where the next intake falls rather than assuming you have missed it.

Open the national enrollment page at seacadets.org (opens in a new tab)

Remember to select The Sullivans Division, Buffalo NY.

Not ready to enroll yet?

Ask whatever you need to ask. Time commitment, cost, what a drill weekend actually looks like, whether it suits your child. A person from the division answers, usually within a week.
